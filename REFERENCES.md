# References for digital preservation and AI-assisted organization

Links checked 2026-11-07. These authoritative sources support the digital preservation, personal digital archives, and AI-risk portions of the guide. They do not validate every local recommendation or establish that this guide is first. Read [METHOD.md](METHOD.md) for the plain-language workflow and [AI-ASSISTED-METHOD.md](AI-ASSISTED-METHOD.md) for the technical version.

## Digital preservation and personal archives

- Digital Preservation Coalition, [Digital Preservation Handbook](https://www.dpconline.org/handbook/) - a practical knowledge base for managing digital resources over time. Useful here for the larger preservation pattern behind inventories, provenance, fixity, metadata, and ongoing care; it informs [METHOD.md](METHOD.md) sections 2, 3, 10, 11, and 13 and the technical method.
- Digital Preservation Coalition, [Getting Started](https://www.dpconline.org/handbook/getting-started) - guidance on rapid assessment, asset registers, multiple copies, fixity checks, recovery tests, and process documentation. These ideas appear in the basic order in [OVERVIEW.md](OVERVIEW.md), the inventory and verification steps in [METHOD.md](METHOD.md), and [SAFETY.md](SAFETY.md).
- Library of Congress, [Preservation](https://www.loc.gov/preservation/) - preservation education and digital preservation resources from the Library of Congress. We use it as broad orientation for treating personal files as material that needs care, access planning, and more than one safe copy; it supports the preservation connection in [METHOD.md](METHOD.md) section 13.
- Library of Congress, [PREMIS](https://www.loc.gov/standards/premis/) - a preservation-metadata standard covering digital objects, events, agents, and rights. Its event and responsibility concepts inform the technical method’s provenance records, approval events, and verification notes.
- National Archives, [Preserving Your Digital Memories](https://www.archives.gov/files/preservation/formats/pdf/preserving-your-digital-memories.pdf) - plain-language guidance on selecting, organizing, copying, and checking personal digital photographs, audio, video, email, and records. It helps keep [OVERVIEW.md](OVERVIEW.md) and [SAFETY.md](SAFETY.md) practical for ordinary personal collections.
- National Archives, [Digital Preservation](https://www.archives.gov/preservation/digital-preservation) - guidance on preservation risks and prioritization. It supports the guide’s advice to match the amount of documentation and checking to the value, risk, and uncertainty of the collection.

## AI governance and human oversight

- NIST, [Artificial Intelligence Risk Management Framework 1.0](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework) - a voluntary framework for managing risks to people, organizations, and society associated with AI. It supports the guide’s separation of roles, human approval, documentation, monitoring, and stop-or-escalate decisions in [SAFETY.md](SAFETY.md) and [AI-ASSISTED-METHOD.md](AI-ASSISTED-METHOD.md).
- NIST, [Artificial Intelligence Risk Management Framework: Generative Artificial Intelligence Profile](https://www.nist.gov/publications/artificial-intelligence-risk-management-framework-generative-artificial-intelligence) - a companion profile describing generative-AI risks and suggested risk-management actions. It helps explain why the guide limits what is sent to a chatbot, records AI assistance, and treats model output as review material rather than authority. The stable DOI is [10.6028/NIST.AI.600-1](https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.AI.600-1).

## How the sources fit together

The preservation sources explain how to protect files and record what happened to them. The AI governance sources explain how to keep people responsible for decisions made with AI assistance. The guide combines those ideas into one small workflow: inventory first, preserve the source and its history, use AI for suggestions, require human approval, make reversible changes, and verify the result. The personal-archiving sources keep that workflow usable for ordinary families and individuals rather than only for institutions.

## Reading and citation policy

For a factual claim, prefer a stable primary or authoritative source and record the date checked. Cite the exact source that supports the claim. Mark local observations, recommendations, and open questions so readers do not mistake them for externally established facts.
