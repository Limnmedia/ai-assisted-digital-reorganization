Before you begin

Safety comes first

Digital organization can cause privacy loss or destroy the only usable copy. Keep the original files safe and keep a person responsible for important decisions.

Protect originals

Keep an untouched source copy. Work from a copy or staging area. Do not delete while you are still guessing.

Protect private information

Do not paste passwords, recovery codes, private keys, or a full personal archive into a chatbot. Redact names and paths before sharing a file list.

Keep human approval

People approve deletion, merging, overwriting, access changes, bulk renaming, and public sharing. AI suggestions are not permission.

Check the result

Compare counts, open sample files, check dates and metadata, and use checksums when useful. Record failures instead of hiding them.

Stop when unsure. Pause if files are missing, a cloud service behaves unexpectedly, the AI conflicts with what the owner knows, or a decision could affect someone’s rights or privacy.

Know what AI can and cannot do

AI is useful for sorting a long inventory, spotting repeated patterns, explaining unfamiliar terms, and suggesting neutral labels or questions. It is not reliable at deciding what a memory means, who owns a file, whether two similar photos are interchangeable, or whether a file is safe to delete. A confident answer is still a suggestion.

A safe first session

  1. Ask the AI to help with a read-only inventory.
  2. Share only a small, redacted sample of filenames or file details.
  3. Ask it to separate facts from guesses and list what is unknown.
  4. Do not let it delete, move, rename, merge, upload, or overwrite anything.
  5. Save useful questions and suggestions beside your inventory.

Before an important change, name the files or group, the proposed change, the reason, and how you can undo it. “Clean this up” is not enough permission for a bulk change.
