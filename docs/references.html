Digital preservation and personal archives

  • Digital Preservation Coalition, Digital Preservation Handbook - a practical knowledge base for managing digital resources over time. Useful here for the larger preservation pattern behind inventories, provenance, fixity, metadata, and ongoing care; it informs the human and technical methods.
  • Digital Preservation Coalition, Getting Started - guidance on rapid assessment, asset registers, multiple copies, fixity checks, recovery tests, and process documentation. These ideas appear in the overview, inventory and verification steps, and safety guide.
  • Library of Congress, Preservation - preservation education and digital preservation resources from the Library of Congress. We use it as broad orientation for treating personal files as material that needs care, access planning, and more than one safe copy.
  • Library of Congress, PREMIS - a preservation-metadata standard covering digital objects, events, agents, and rights. Its event and responsibility concepts inform the technical method’s provenance records, approval events, and verification notes.
  • National Archives, Preserving Your Digital Memories - plain-language guidance on selecting, organizing, copying, and checking personal digital photographs, audio, video, email, and records. It helps keep the overview and safety guide practical for ordinary personal collections.
  • National Archives, Digital Preservation - guidance on preservation risks and prioritization. It supports matching the amount of documentation and checking to the value, risk, and uncertainty of the collection.

AI governance and human oversight

  • NIST, Artificial Intelligence Risk Management Framework 1.0 - a voluntary framework for managing AI risks. It supports the guide’s separation of roles, human approval, documentation, monitoring, and stop-or-escalate decisions.
  • NIST, Artificial Intelligence Risk Management Framework: Generative Artificial Intelligence Profile - a companion profile describing generative-AI risks and suggested risk-management actions. It helps explain why the guide limits what is sent to a chatbot, records AI assistance, and treats model output as review material rather than authority. The stable DOI is 10.6028/NIST.AI.600-1.

How the sources fit together

The preservation sources explain how to protect files and record what happened to them. The AI governance sources explain how to keep people responsible for decisions made with AI assistance. The guide combines those ideas into one small workflow: inventory first, preserve the source and its history, use AI for suggestions, require human approval, make reversible changes, and verify the result. The personal-archiving sources keep that workflow usable for ordinary families and individuals rather than only for institutions.

Reading and citation policy
